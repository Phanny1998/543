The four, together

Demand-forecast updating, order batching and shortage gaming are three of the ways a supply chain turns a steady demand into an unsteady order. The bullwhip effect is the name for what they add up to.

  • Bullwhip effectParents do not change how many nappies they use, yet the factory’s orders swing wildly.
  • Demand-forecast updatingDemand rises by 1 unit. The retailer orders 3 more, and the factory 9 more.
  • Order batchingConsumption is a steady 6 a week. The orders arrive as 18, nothing, nothing, 18.
  • Shortage gamingCustomers order double, because they expect to be given half.

Orders can change far more than consumption—even when consumption barely changes or stays completely steady.

When things start moving quickly, ask three things

  • 1Are we close to a limit?
  • 2What is really behind the signal I am seeing?
  • 3Has enough time passed for earlier actions to take effect?

And the habit underneath them. Think in curves rather than straight lines, expect amplification, and check whether your own reaction is calming the system down or feeding it.
